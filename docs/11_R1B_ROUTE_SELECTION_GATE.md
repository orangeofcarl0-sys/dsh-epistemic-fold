# R1-B Route Selection Gate

**Status: CLOSED.** Baseline `12a5842`; R1-A `ae37d44`; R1-B `7c2e21d`.
Every number below is machine-generated by `tests/r1b-roi.spec.ts` and is
reproducible keylessly (`npx vitest run tests/r1b-roi.spec.ts`).

This gate exists to answer docs/11 §27's question — *what should we build
next?* — from measured ROI rather than from milestone numbering. Its output is
a decision, not a feature.

---

## 1. What dominates context cost is regime-dependent

Measured across W1–W5 at 64 steps. Shares are of total prompt tokens.

| Workload | Folds | Total tokens | Dominant source | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| W1 narrative-heavy | 18 | 58,342 | `checkpoint-framing` | 51.1% |
| W2 state-rich | 3 | 47,112 | `raw-assistant` | 41.3% |
| W3 tool-heavy | 31 | 115,677 | `checkpoint-framing` | 56.6% |
| W4 recall-heavy | 5 | 44,251 | `recall` | 31.3% |
| W5 multi-agent | 27 | 90,455 | `checkpoint-framing` | 56.6% |

The same workload flips its dominant source when fold pressure changes:

| W1 regime | Folds | `checkpoint-framing` | raw history |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| aggressive (`thresholdRatio` 0.15) | 50 | 74.4% | 14.9% |
| realistic (`thresholdRatio` 0.6) | 4 | 5.2% | 94.0% |

**Consequence for the project.** R0-C's single narrative fixture could not see
this, and any report quoting one of these numbers alone would be a threshold
artifact. Attribution must always be reported with its fold regime.

## 2. The structural finding: the frozen-prefix feedback loop

Two measurements, each independently gated:

1. **~87% of a leaf checkpoint node is fixed overhead.** A node prices at 151
   tokens: 13 state + 6 rationale + ~132 repeated preamble and wrapper. The
   framing scales linearly with fold count while carrying no state.

2. **EF folds strictly more often than Basic — 51 vs 21 folds** on an
   identical workload *and identical digest text*, so this is architectural,
   not a narrative-length artifact. At a tight threshold that is 245,885 vs
   73,878 total tokens, a **3.3× gap** on the same conversation.

**Root cause.** EF may never re-fold a frozen checkpoint (plan §13), so the
frozen prefix is monotonically non-decreasing. Once that prefix alone exceeds
the pressure threshold, *every* subsequent step is over threshold, each fold
can only compact the newly appended tail, and one checkpoint is produced per
step — each paying the full preamble. Measured: the frozen prefix reaches
7,550 tokens against a 2,400-token threshold.

This is a genuine architectural cost of the frontier invariant, not a bug in
it. It is the cost of "frozen checkpoints are never re-folded," and it is
exactly what the R1-D rebase policy exists to amortize.

## 3. Counterfactual upper bounds

Measured at 64 steps, aggressive regime, as a fraction of total prompt tokens.
These are **upper bounds under stated idealizations**, never achievements.

| Candidate | W1 | W2 | W3 | W4 | W5 | Risk | Kind |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Adaptive Root | 77.9% | 23.2% | 74.3% | 39.3% | 76.6% | low | policy change |
| Framing dedup | 70.8% | 15.9% | 66.9% | 37.6% | 69.1% | low | policy change |
| M5 generational | 63.6% | 33.4% | 59.3% | 37.5% | 61.4% | high | new mechanism |
| M1 ingress | 0.0% | 0.0% | 19.5% | 0.0% | 16.6% | high | new mechanism |
| Delta Leaf | 7.0% | 16.6% | 6.6% | 3.7% | 6.8% | medium | new mechanism |

Two rows carry most of the decision:

- **M1 is worthless where there is no tool output** (0.0% on W1 and W2) and
  modest where there is (~19.5% on W3). An M1 decision made from a single
  workload would have been wrong in either direction.
- **Delta Leaf, the mechanism R1 was expected to justify, is the weakest
  candidate on every workload.** The full-state payload it removes is 2–11%
  of spend; the recurring framing it does *not* address is the larger cost.

## 4. Decision

**Delta Leaf is NOT productionized.** Its measured upper bound (3.7–16.6%)
does not justify a new correctness seam at medium implementation risk, and it
addresses the smaller half of the checkpoint cost. Per docs/11 §26, the gate
before writing production Delta Leaf is exactly this table, and it does not
pass.

**Routed to R1-D: provider-aware amortized root.** Highest measured bound
(23.2–77.9%), lowest risk, and — decisively — a *policy change over existing
machinery* rather than a new mechanism. It requires no new fold mode, no new
state representation, and no new way to lose state. It also directly targets
the structural loop in §2: a rebase is the only mechanism that can shrink the
frozen prefix.

**Framing deduplication is recorded as a follow-up, not a stage.** Its bound
is comparable to R1-D's and its risk is low, but it changes the checkpoint
surface format, so it belongs behind the same evidence gate that R1-D must
pass. It is not started here.

**M5 generational merge is deferred.** High bound (33–64%) but high risk and a
new mechanism; docs/11 §31 routes to it only if hierarchy still fails to scale
*after* Delta and Root work. Delta was rejected, so that precondition is not
met yet.

## 5. What this gate does not establish

- **No behavioral evidence.** These are token economics, not task success.
  A 78% cheaper policy that loses 5% of task success may be worthless. Live
  paired validation remains ⏸ OPEN (requires provider credentials) and is
  what R1-E records; no production default may be flipped on economics alone.
- **No provider cache realization.** The keyless tier has no provider to
  report `cacheReadTokens`, so realization is treated as a parameter, not a
  measurement. `eval/src/provider-telemetry.ts` computes it when a live run
  supplies usage; until then every money figure is conditional on `h`.
- **Synthetic window.** All runs use a 16,000-token window to force pressure
  at tractable step counts. Absolute costs are not production-scale; the
  comparisons and the structural finding are.
