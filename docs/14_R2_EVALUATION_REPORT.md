# R2 Evaluation Report — Baseline-Dominant Context Compression

> Generated by `tests/r2e-dominance.spec.ts` (matrix) and the R2 stage suites.
> Window 16,000 tokens · 64 steps · keyless · realization h = 0.910 (measured
> live in R1, not assumed).

Baseline: R1 merged at `78c347a`. R2 commits: R2-A `8ad757b`, R2-B `ce8a1c4`,
R2-C `919cbd0`, R2-D `349d93f`.

---

## 1. The objective, and the verdict

R2's exit condition is a combination:

```
Q_EF >= Q_Basic - ε     AND     C_EF < C_Basic
```

**Verdict: the first half holds, the second does not.**

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| No worse in quality (BQR ≥ 1) | ✅ 40/40 cells (see §5 for what this can and cannot mean) |
| Cheaper than Basic (BCR < 1) | ❌ **0/40 cells** |
| **Gate P (parity)** | ❌ **FAIL** |
| **Gate D (dominance)** | ❌ **FAIL** |

**Mean BCR = 1.451** across the full matrix. On the recommended DeepSeek Flash
profile specifically:

| Policy | Mean BCR | Per-workload |
|---|---:|---|
| E0 legacy | 1.276 | 1.242, 1.186, 1.357, 1.164, 1.431 |
| **E3 full (R2)** | **1.149** | 1.090, 1.174, 1.123, 1.207, 1.151 |

So R2 delivered a large improvement — **BCR 1.276 → 1.149, a 38% reduction in
the excess cost over Basic** — and still did not reach the goal. EF remains
about 15% more expensive than Basic on the profile where it looks best.

## 2. What R2 fixed, and by how much

Three mechanisms, each measured against the arm before it:

| Stage | Mechanism | Measured effect (W1, 64 steps) |
|---|---|---|
| R2-A | Pressure attribution | diagnostic only; exposed the loop |
| R2-B | Economic leaf admission | folds 50 → 16, but **total 236K → 428K (regression)** |
| R2-C | Rebase handoff | **total → 103K**, peak 7,896 → 2,547, frozen 7,550 → 1,057 |
| R2-D | Checkpoint surface diet | checkpoint body 41 → 23 tokens (−44%) |

R2-C is the load-bearing fix: it made EF **2.3× cheaper than its own legacy
mode** and cut peak context by 68%. R2-B alone was a 1.8× regression, and that
negative result is pinned as a test so it cannot be forgotten.

## 3. Why EF is still not cheaper — the structural cause

Decomposing each arm's measured warm/cold split on the Flash profile:

| Workload | Basic cold | EF cold | cold Δ | Basic warm | EF warm | warm Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| W1 | 38,983 | 39,888 | +905 | 34,643 | 61,561 | **+26,918** |
| W2 | 10,619 | 11,932 | +1,313 | 72,720 | 90,283 | +17,563 |
| W3 | 86,317 | 88,120 | +1,803 | 960 | 81,533 | **+80,573** |
| W4 | 18,417 | 20,012 | +1,595 | 54,219 | 80,943 | +26,724 |
| W5 | 58,653 | 63,123 | +4,470 | 30,815 | 75,655 | +44,840 |

**EF's cold-token overhead is nearly zero (+905 to +4,470). Its entire
disadvantage is warm tokens: it carries 1.5× to 85× more of them.**

The cause is the frontier invariant. Basic re-folds its single checkpoint, so
its frozen prefix stays small and constant. EF may never re-fold a frozen
checkpoint, so each fold appends to a chain that only a rebase can collapse —
and a rebase makes everything cold again (measured: greedy rebasing drove warm
tokens to 1,376 and was *worse*).

This is a genuine tension with no free resolution:

- **Few rebases** → the chain grows → more warm tokens than Basic.
- **Many rebases** → the prefix keeps changing → warm tokens stop being warm.

A rebase cadence sweep on W1 confirmed the interior optimum is flat and still
above 1:

| Rebase every N steps | 0 (never) | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BCR | 1.607 | 1.198 | 1.137 | 1.101 | 1.096 |

Even the best cadence lands at 1.096 — above 1. **Cadence tuning cannot reach
the objective.**

## 4. What would actually be required

Holding everything else fixed, the warm-token gap alone accounts for the
shortfall: pricing EF as if it carried Basic's warm-token count yields
**BCR = 1.021** instead of 1.089 on W1. So the remaining work is not "compress
more" — it is **stop carrying a growing chain of checkpoint bodies**.

Three candidates, none of which R2 implemented, in the order the evidence
supports them:

1. **Framing deduplication (L3 in the R1 directive).** Each checkpoint still
   repeats a ~116-token preamble. R2-D removed the *empty sections* but not the
   per-checkpoint preamble, which lives in DSH Basic's `frameSummary` and would
   need a core seam. Measured upper bound from R1: 66.9–70.8% of tokens on the
   fold-heavy workloads.
2. **Delta leaves.** R1 measured this as the weakest candidate (3.7–16.6%) and
   R2's decomposition does not overturn that: the state payload is a small part
   of the warm excess. It remains rejected.
3. **Bounded checkpoint chain.** Cap the number of frozen checkpoints and force
   a merge rather than a rebase, so the prefix stops growing without becoming
   cold. This is the M5 generational fold the R1 directive deferred — and R2's
   data is the first evidence that it targets the actual bottleneck.

## 5. What "BQR = 1" does and does not mean

Quality is **not measurable in the keyless tier**, so BQR is held at 1.0
throughout this matrix. That is not a measurement of EF's quality; it is the
statement that this tier cannot produce one.

### The live re-validation that was run (added after §5 was first written)

The gap flagged below was partially closed with a live suite
(`tests/r2-live-behavior.spec.ts`, opt-in, `npm run eval:r2-live`). It compares
three arms — Basic, EF legacy, and the full R2 policy — on identical scenarios
with machine-scored probes, at a far deeper compression ratio than R1 tested:
**~50K tokens of history folded into a single checkpoint of 220–666 tokens.**

| Arm | Probes passed | Checkpoint tokens |
|---|---:|---:|
| B1 Basic | 15/15 (100%) | 572 |
| E0 EF legacy | 15/15 (100%) | 574 |
| **E2 EF R2 policy** | **15/15 (100%)** | **220** |

**Result: no behavioral regression from the R2 policy.** It answered every
probe correctly while producing a checkpoint 2.6× smaller than legacy EF's
(220 vs 574 tokens) and 2.6× smaller than Basic's — the R2-D diet and the
admission policy visible in real data.

Two caveats, both material:

1. **This is one deep fold, not a chain of folds and rebases.** The harness
   builds a session and drives the pressure path over it, so the first call
   folds everything foldable and later calls find nothing to do. A genuine
   multi-fold/multi-rebase test needs incremental growth with a fold between
   turns. **The rebase path specifically remains behaviorally unvalidated.**
2. **The sample is small** (5 replicates × 3 facts × 3 arms). It rules out gross
   regression; it cannot establish parity with a confidence interval.

The suite carries a vacuity guard that FAILS when no checkpoint lands. That
guard earned its place immediately: the first version of this suite reported
100% for all three arms while folding nothing at all, because the adapter's own
reported window (131K) overrode the harness setting and the pressure threshold
was never reached.

### The remaining honest reading

The only other behavioral evidence in the project is R1's live tier: **EF 32/32
vs Basic 31/32** over 64 paired trials — also a null result. So the quality half
of R2's gate is: *no evidence of degradation, at two compression depths*, not
*evidence of parity*. The largest open risk is now narrower than it was — the
R2 surface format and admission policy have been exercised against a real model
— but the rebase policy has not.

## 6. Correctness

Unchanged and not traded: ALR = 0, SSR = 0, ProvenanceCoverage = 100%,
CrossSessionLeak = 0, ExactRecallMismatch = 0, CriticalConstraintViolation = 0.
Every R2 change is fold-policy only; none touches the bundle, state, authority
or recall layers. Attribution still reconciles exactly to the metered total on
every arm, and pressure still decomposes exactly, both asserted in the stage
suites.

Defaults remain `leafAdmission: legacy` and `rootPolicy: legacy`. **No
production default was flipped**, because the exit gate did not pass.

## 7. Status

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| Fold-every-step regime eliminated | ✅ folds 50 → 24, loop broken |
| Peak context reduced | ✅ 7,896 → 2,489 (−68%) |
| Checkpoint body dieted | ✅ −44% per checkpoint (220 vs 574 tokens live) |
| R2 policy behaviorally re-validated | ✅ no regression at one deep fold (15/15 vs 15/15) |
| **BCR < 1 (the R2 objective)** | ❌ **not achieved (1.149 on the best profile)** |
| Rebase path behaviorally validated | ❌ **not done — needs incremental multi-fold harness** |
| Production default flip | ❌ blocked on BCR < 1 |

R2 is **not complete**. The fold loop is fixed and the cost is nearly halved
relative to its own starting point, but EF still does not beat Basic on price,
and the reason is now precisely identified rather than suspected.
